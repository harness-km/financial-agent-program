# Week NN — Short focus phrase

Copy this folder to `week-NN/` and fill it in. This is the week page: the thing a
learner reads to decide whether they are ready for the session, and what they
should come back to when they have lost the thread mid-week.

It is not the assignment. The assignment is `spec.md`, which is precise enough to
prompt from and becomes the GitHub issue. This file is the orientation around it,
and it should be readable in two minutes.

Keep the five headings. Replace every sentence.

## What changes this week

One paragraph. What the system can do at the end of this week that it could not
at the start, in terms somebody who missed last week would still follow.

The test for this section: could a learner decide from it whether they are
behind? If it describes activity rather than a change in what exists, no.

## Why it is in this order

Two or three sentences on why this week sits here and not somewhere else — what
it depends on from earlier weeks, and what later weeks cannot be built without
it.

This section exists because the most common mid-programme question is "why are we
doing this now", and the answer is almost always load-bearing rather than
arbitrary. A week whose position cannot be justified is a week in the wrong
place.

## The session

What happens in the two hours, in order, so people know what they are walking
into. Name the demo, because the demo is usually the thing people remember.

On a sprint-boundary week, say that the consultant session follows and name the
artifact it reasons about.

## What you will have afterwards

The concrete leftovers: files, a number, a plot, a committed row. Link to
`spec.md` for what must be true of them.

Not "an understanding of X". Something that exists on disk or in a pull request.

## If you are behind

Where to pick up. The known-good branch for this week, what to run to check your
environment, and the one thing you must have working before the next session even
if you skip the rest.

Every week has this section, and it stays honest: there is usually exactly one
piece that later weeks cannot route around, and naming it is more useful than
pretending the whole assignment is mandatory.
